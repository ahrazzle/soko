\begin{abstract}
\noindent When large language model (LLM) agents author both a combinatorial solver and its accompanying verification script, passing unit tests frequently masks silent soundness failures. During a multi-agent evaluation on the \href{https://soko.banteg.xyz/frontier/}{Sokoban Eval Frontier Challenge} across Jonah Ostroff's 66-room \href{https://sites.math.washington.edu/~ostroff/puzzles/Heroes_of_Sokoban.html}{\emph{Heroes of Sokoban} trilogy}, an initial agent-authored Model Context Protocol (MCP) server (\texttt{soko-verified-mcp v0.1.0}) passed all six baseline tests and reproduced a live record 84-key trajectory on Room \texttt{III-20} (surpassing the 85-key holdout target and 145-key model baseline). Yet cross-agent adversarial review uncovered six soundness and generality defects (\texttt{F-01}--\texttt{F-06}), including two critical verification failures: (1) an optimistic boolean initialization that silently emitted \texttt{VERIFIED} certificates from a single interpreter whenever C++20 compilation was absent (\texttt{F-01}), and (2) lossy tag-only transposition-table pruning in lower-bound search that suffered \textbf{22,658 live 64-bit hash collisions} within 35 seconds across $2.766 \times 10^{7}$ exact states on Room \texttt{III-14} (\texttt{F-04}).

To eliminate these failure modes structurally, \texttt{soko-verified-mcp} (\texttt{v0.2.0}--\texttt{v0.3.0}) introduces a domain-general, 16-tool Formal-Systems MCP Kernel (\texttt{formal\_*} and \texttt{soko\_*}). The kernel enforces AST-sandboxed pure execution over exact integers and rationals, blocks alpha-renamed copy-paste tautologies via structural AST skeleton hashing, mandates negative-control rejection and planted-mutation kill gates before unlocking search, and restricts lower-bound certificates to machine-checked Quotient Graph Homomorphisms, Inductive Potentials, and Houdini greatest-fixpoint invariants. Across three evaluation domains, the hardened server certifies the 84-key chiral-mirror record on \texttt{III-20}, raises the collision-free exact-state lower bound on \texttt{III-14} from 9 to 35 across $1.108 \times 10^{8}$ verified transitions via a $3.97 \times 10^{7}$-state 2-Atom Quotient Pattern Database, extracts a 5-step split-brain counterexample in an unfenced dual-gateway lease protocol before proving global safety across all 486 syntactic states under monotonic epoch fencing, and closes a precedence-constrained setup-cost scheduling benchmark at $\mathrm{LB} = \mathrm{UB} = 19$. Following Foundation-0 epistemic rules, this paper separates the genuine systems contribution---mechanical anti-tautology gates for agentic verification---from the classical nature of the small-state protocol and scheduling benchmarks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Verification Illusions in Agent-Authored Solvers}

Combinatorial planning benchmarks expose a recurring asymmetry between discovering candidate trajectories and certifying mathematical claims. On the \href{https://soko.banteg.xyz/frontier/}{Sokoban Eval Frontier Challenge} (\href{https://banteg.xyz/posts/sokoban/}{\emph{Eight moves to the exit}}), frontier language models evaluated across the 66 rooms of Jonah Ostroff's PuzzleScript trilogy---\href{https://www.puzzlescript.net/editor.html?hack=6860122}{\emph{Heroes of Sokoban Part I}}, \href{https://www.puzzlescript.net/editor.html?hack=6910207}{\emph{Part II: Monsters}}, and \href{https://www.puzzlescript.net/editor.html?hack=7072276}{\emph{Part III: The Bard and The Druid}}---achieved proven optimality in 44 of 66 rooms with a best composite segment total of 3,819 inputs against a 3,000-key trilogy lower-bound floor and 3,064 keys across the 22 holdout routes \cite{banteg2026sokoban,sokobanteg2026frontier}.

Unlike classical single-agent Imabayashi Sokoban, the trilogy composes six coupled hero movement laws under PuzzleScript's synchronous rule-rewriting engine \cite{ostroff2026heroes}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Fighter (\texttt{F}/\texttt{1}):} Pushes colinear chains of arbitrarily many \texttt{Moveable} entities (crates \texttt{*}, dormant heroes \texttt{1..6}, and monsters) whenever an empty cell terminates the ray.
  \item \textbf{Thief (\texttt{T}/\texttt{2}):} Steps into an empty cell and pulls one orthogonally trailing \texttt{Moveable}.
  \item \textbf{Wizard (\texttt{W}/\texttt{3}):} Fires a projective warpball that swaps positions with the first \texttt{Moveable} struck, or advances one empty cell if striking a \texttt{Static} barrier (\texttt{\#}, closed doors, monster gates \texttt{M}, or Part III board edges).
  \item \textbf{Priest (\texttt{P}/\texttt{4}):} Protects itself and orthogonally adjacent heroes---both when active (\texttt{P}) and when dormant (\texttt{4})---against simultaneous \texttt{Late}-phase Goblin/Dragon \texttt{Dying} rules and Druid petrification.
  \item \textbf{Bard (\texttt{B}/\texttt{5}, Part III):} Projects a $5\times 5$ Chebyshev \texttt{Note} aura ($\max(|\Delta x|, |\Delta y|) \le 2$) computed at the \emph{conclusion of the preceding turn}; on movement, Bard and all charmed \texttt{Moveable} objects translate rigidly to a simultaneous fixed point, reverting via \texttt{Cancel} if Bard itself cannot advance.
  \item \textbf{Druid (\texttt{D}/\texttt{6}, Part III):} Transmutes adjacent crates or goblins into walls ($\{\texttt{*},\texttt{O}\}\to\texttt{\#}$), walls (including outer boundary walls) into crates ($\texttt{\#}\to\texttt{*}$), and unshielded dormant heroes into walls, where transmuted walls count as \texttt{Weighing} on yellow and purple pressure switches.
\end{enumerate}

When autonomous coding agents translate rich rule systems into fast C++ or Python search engines, subtle semantic drift is common: evaluating Bard's aura in Manhattan rather than Chebyshev norm, computing charm masks before rather than after a move, cycling \texttt{Action} (\texttt{A}) in \texttt{OBJECTS} order rather than \texttt{LEGEND} order, or resolving Goblin combat sequentially rather than simultaneously. More dangerously, when the same agent writes the test harness, passing tests can create an \emph{illusion of formal verification}. This paper documents what survived inside a live record-setting MCP server (\texttt{v0.1.0}) and how a minimal, self-auditing MCP protocol (\texttt{v0.3.0}) mechanically eliminates those failure modes.

\subsection{Anatomy of Two Silent Verification Hazards}

Two defects in Table~\ref{tab:audit} illustrate why code review by independent verification seats is indispensable even when tests pass:

\textbf{1. Optimistic Boolean Defaults (\texttt{F-01}).} In \texttt{v0.1.0}, \texttt{verify\_route\_dual} ran the Python reference oracle, attempted to run the C++20 binary, and set \texttt{differential\_match = True} \emph{outside} the conditional block checking whether C++20 succeeded. On any container lacking \texttt{g++}/\texttt{clang++}, or on any grid exceeding the fixed 512-cell C++ buffer, the server silently skipped differential replay while returning \texttt{"epistemic\_status": "VERIFIED"}. All six unit tests passed because the authoring environment had \texttt{g++} installed and tested rooms with $W\cdot H \le 195$. Initializing \texttt{differential\_match = False} (fail-closed) and pairing \texttt{PuzzleScriptOracle} with a zero-shared-code 1D integer bitmask interpreter (\texttt{FlatBitmaskOracle}) guarantees genuine two-engine replay in pure Python and three-engine replay whenever C++20 is compiled.

\textbf{2. Birthday-Paradox Hash Collisions in Exact Lower-Bound Provers (\texttt{F-04}).} Heuristic game engines routinely prune transposition subtrees on 48-bit or 64-bit hash tags without storing full states, because rare false prunes scarcely change playing strength. In a \emph{mathematical lower-bound prover}, however, a single false prune invalidates the proof: if state $s_{\text{opt}}$ on an optimal path collides in hash tag with an earlier state $s_{\text{dead}}$ recorded with greater remaining budget, the prover prunes $s_{\text{opt}}$ and can emit an unsound lower bound $\mathrm{OPT} > B$.

For $N$ distinct visited states hashed into $b$-bit tags (or an open-addressed table of $M = 2^{18}$ slots), birthday-paradox collisions scale quadratically as $\mathbb{E}[\text{collisions}] \approx N^{2}/2^{b+1}$, while slot-index collisions occur continuously at high load factors. Instrumenting full \texttt{DynState} and 112-bit \texttt{Packed14} equality checks on Room \texttt{III-14} revealed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{11 live hash collisions prevented} in a 6-second IDA* run ($802,816$ nodes; $3,928,470$ edge checks) in the $2^{18}$-slot table;
  \item \textbf{1,014 live full-64-bit hash collisions prevented} in a 10-second multi-core bucketed A* run ($4.14 \times 10^{6}$ transitions); and
  \item \textbf{22,658 live full-64-bit hash collisions prevented} in a 35-second exact-state A* run ($2.766 \times 10^{7}$ unique \texttt{Packed14} states; $1.108 \times 10^{8}$ transitions).
\end{itemize}
Every one of those 22,658 events was a genuine 64-bit hash collision between two distinct reachable configurations of Room \texttt{III-14}. Full-state comparison reduces false hash-collision pruning to identically zero.

\subsection{AST Sandboxing and Anti-Tautology Skeleton Hashing}

When prompted to write two independent checkers, an unguarded LLM agent frequently duplicates its specification and renames local identifiers (\texttt{state} $\to$ \texttt{x}, \texttt{action} $\to$ \texttt{mv}), yielding correlated bugs with zero implementation diversity. In \texttt{formal\_define\_system}, both \texttt{spec\_code} and \texttt{impl\_code} must pass two static checks prior to execution:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deterministic Exact-Arithmetic Sandbox (\texttt{validate\_\allowbreak pure\_\allowbreak ast}):} Forbids \texttt{import}, I/O, \texttt{global}/\allowbreak\texttt{nonlocal}, async/generator constructs, bare \texttt{except:}, private/dunder attribute access, and---by default---all IEEE-754 floating-point literals and \texttt{/} division, exposing \texttt{fractions.Fraction} so rational potentials and step costs remain exact across architectures.
  \item \textbf{Alpha-Normalized Skeleton Distinctness:} An AST canonicalizer strips docstrings and renames all non-interface local identifiers in deterministic preorder ($v_{0}, v_{1}, \dots$), computing $\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}(M) = \mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{dump}(\alpha(M)))$. Whenever $\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}(\texttt{spec\_code}) = \mathcal{H}_{\alpha}(\texttt{impl\_code})$, registration aborts with \texttt{REJECTED\_IDENTICAL\_AST\_SKELETON}, requiring genuinely distinct algorithms and state encodings (such as \texttt{frozenset}/tuple records in \texttt{SPEC} vs.\ bit-packed integers in \texttt{IMPL}, compared via an observation projection $\pi_{\text{obs}}$).
\end{enumerate}

Registration also mandates 100\% rejection of at least one \texttt{must\_fail\_controls} negative trace, 100\% kill rate across supplied \texttt{planted\_mutations} (adapted from \texttt{tests/plant\_bugs.py} in \href{https://github.com/Proven-Commons/semver}{Proven-Commons SemVer} \cite{provencommons2026semver} and \href{https://github.com/Proven-Commons/protobuf}{Proven-Commons Protobuf} \cite{provencommons2026protobuf}), and zero observational divergence across bounded breadth-first differential state-space fuzzing.

\subsection{Zero-Trust Lower Bounds via Quotient Graph Homomorphisms}

Hand-crafted lower-bound heuristics written by agents regularly violate step consistency ($h(s) > c(s,a,s') + h(s')$) on edge-case transitions---for example, in Room \texttt{III-14}, Druid transmuting an interior crate into an off-switch wall caused a candidate heuristic to drop by 2 in one keypress. Rather than trusting heuristic code, \texttt{formal\_certify\_lower\_bound} machine-checks lower bounds via finite Quotient Homomorphisms or Inductive Potentials.

\begin{proposition}[Quotient Homomorphism Lower Bounds]
\label{prop:quotient}
Let $\mathcal{M} = (\mathcal{S}, s_{0}, \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{act}}, T, c, G)$ be a transition system with non-negative step costs $c(s,a,s') \ge 0$ and goal set $G \subseteq \mathcal{S}$, and let $\pi : \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{Q}$ map concrete states onto a finite quotient $\mathcal{Q}$. If an abstract directed graph $(\mathcal{Q}, E_{\mathcal{Q}}, w_{\mathcal{Q}})$ with $w_{\mathcal{Q}} \ge 0$ and abstract goals $G_{\mathcal{Q}} \subseteq \mathcal{Q}$ satisfies:
\begin{align}
  \forall (s, a, s') \in T: \quad & (\pi(s), \pi(s')) \in E_{\mathcal{Q}} \;\land\; w_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s), \pi(s')) \le c(s, a, s'), \label{eq:hom1}\\
  \forall s \in G: \quad & \pi(s) \in G_{\mathcal{Q}}, \label{eq:hom2}
\end{align}
then exact reverse-Dijkstra shortest-path distances $d_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s), G_{\mathcal{Q}})$ satisfy $d_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s), G_{\mathcal{Q}}) \le d_{\mathcal{M}}(s, G)$ for all $s \in \mathcal{S}$ and obey step consistency $d_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s), G_{\mathcal{Q}}) \le c(s,a,s') + d_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s'), G_{\mathcal{Q}})$ across every concrete transition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every concrete goal-reaching path $s = s_{0} \xrightarrow{a_{1}} s_{1} \cdots \xrightarrow{a_{k}} s_{k} \in G$ projects under $\pi$ to an abstract walk $\pi(s_{0}) \to \pi(s_{1}) \cdots \to \pi(s_{k}) \in G_{\mathcal{Q}}$ by \eqref{eq:hom1}--\eqref{eq:hom2} of total weight $\sum_{i=1}^{k} w_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s_{i-1}), \pi(s_{i})) \le \sum_{i=1}^{k} c(s_{i-1}, a_{i}, s_{i})$. Minimizing over all walks in $\mathcal{Q}$ establishes admissibility $d_{\mathcal{Q}}(\pi(s), G_{\mathcal{Q}}) \le d_{\mathcal{M}}(s, G)$, and triangle inequality of exact shortest-path distances in $(\mathcal{Q}, E_{\mathcal{Q}}, w_{\mathcal{Q}})$ yields step consistency on every concrete edge.
\end{proof}

In \texttt{v0.3.0}, \texttt{formal\_synthesize\_invariant} adds two greatest-fixpoint engines following Flanagan and Leino's Houdini algorithm \cite{flanagan2001houdini}: (1) \texttt{houdini\_safety}, computing the maximal inductive conjunction $I^{*} \subseteq \mathcal{P}$ satisfying $I^{*}(s_{0})$ and $I^{*}(s) \Rightarrow I^{*}(s')$ (emitting \texttt{PROVEN} whenever an exhaustive syntactic state enumerator \texttt{universe\_code} closes $\mathcal{S}$), and (2) \texttt{deadlock\_trap}, synthesizing goal-disjoint forward-closed trap unions certifying $d_{\mathcal{M}}(s, G) = +\infty$.

\subsection{Chiral-Mirror Symmetry Breaking on Room \texttt{III-20} (\texorpdfstring{$87 \to 84$}{87 to 84} Keys)}

Room \texttt{III-20} (\emph{Goblin Corridor}, $15\times 9$, 12 Goblins, 5 heroes) possesses an exact vertical reflection $\sigma_{y}(x,y) = (x, 8-y)$ across its static dungeon walls, goblins, and goals, yet positions Fighter (\texttt{1}) and Wizard (\texttt{W}) on row $y=2$ while Thief (\texttt{2}) and Druid (\texttt{6}) start on row $y=6$. Because the \texttt{Action} (\texttt{A}) key advances active control in fixed cyclic \texttt{LEGEND} order $\tau = (1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 6 \to 1)$, $\sigma_{y}$ fails to commute with $\tau$: reflecting a trajectory across $y \leftrightarrow 8-y$ exchanges upper and lower spatial corridors without inverting hero-switch distances. Whereas the prior 87-key Frontier record walked Priest (\texttt{4}) upward to $(4,1)$ and expended a 4-corner Wizard teleportation cycle plus triple \texttt{A}-switches to bring Druid up from row 6, reflecting the seed trajectory across $\sigma_{y}$ into a role-agnostic spatial Tube Beam routes Priest immediately downward (\texttt{ADDDRR}), where Thief and Druid already reside. Iterative tube polishing yields two distinct triple-oracle-verified \textbf{84-key} routes (\texttt{...AUUR}, \texttt{sha256: eecbea96b091}; and \texttt{...AURU}, \texttt{sha256: 4edff1ad8c90}), beating Banteg's 85-key holdout and lowering the 66-room trilogy shortest-known sum from 3,064 to 3,063.

\subsection{38-Atom Matter Conservation and Column-6 Blow-Off on Room \texttt{III-14}}

In Room \texttt{III-14} ($14\times 7$, Bard + Druid, nine purple switches $\{4,5,6\}\times\{2,3,4\}$ guarding two goals behind twelve purple doors), every non-deadlock Druid transmutation either converts a wall into a crate ($\texttt{\#}\to\texttt{*}$) or a crate into a wall ($\texttt{*}\to\texttt{\#}$). Total weighing matter is therefore invariant across all solvable states:
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{popcnt}(\text{crates}(s)) + \operatorname{popcnt}(\text{walls}(s)) = 38 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k_{\text{created}}(s) = \operatorname{popcnt}(\text{INIT\_WALLS} \setminus \text{walls}(s)).
\end{equation}
In \texttt{v0.3.0}, \texttt{gem-nexus} built a $3.97 \times 10^{7}$-state \textbf{2-Atom Quotient Homomorphism PDB} (\texttt{PairAtom\allowbreak QuotientPDB}) tracking unordered pairs of created weighing atoms over $\{\text{crate}@v, \text{wall}@v, \text{UNBORN}, \text{SPARE}\}$ jointly with Bard/Druid coordinates and active turn. Crucially, the abstract transition graph admits \emph{wall-blocked differential Bard translations}---including step 25 of the 48-key witness where charmed crate $(2,5)$ abuts wall $(2,6)$ while charmed crate $(5,1) \to (5,2)$ translates downward---satisfying Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient} with \textbf{zero} step-consistency violations across $1.108 \times 10^{8}$ concrete edges and certifying $\mathrm{OPT}(\texttt{III-14}) \ge 35$ in 35 seconds. \textbf{Calibration of Scientific Novelty (No Overclaiming).} Per the \href{https://github.com/Foundation-0/incentre-lattices/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md}{Foundation-0 Charter}, we state plainly what these non-puzzle evaluations do and do not show. Monotonic fencing tokens are classical distributed-systems primitives \cite{lamport1978time,kleppmann2016fencing}, and conjunctive fixpoint synthesis over 486 states or quotient shortest-path closure over 32 states is computationally small. These two tests are \emph{not} claimed as new theorems in consensus or scheduling theory.
